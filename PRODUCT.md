# Change

A public, independent history of computing and Internet security, from the 1980s through November 6, 2026. Readers remember these practices and want to explore what changed, why, and where the change actually applied. The user requested a researched, visually engaging, intuitive, mobile-responsive website, published to pete-wai/change on GitHub Pages.

Each event connects a once-deployed practice with a threat and a documented response. Retired features and enforced defaults lead; adopted defenses and incomplete transitions are clearly distinguished. Forty-six selected entries emerged from an initial 110-lead survey, three Luna research passes, lead verification, and separate Sol factual and prose reviews. Counts are an editorial selection, never an exhaustive history or a measure of risk.

Visitor tasks: revisit a familiar event; compare the old behavior and the response; browse chronologically or by topic; follow original evidence. Public summaries are short recollections, with factual limits and detailed chronologies available on demand. Every entry has source links. Research and reviews remain in the repository, outside the built site.

No accounts, analytics, backend, paid services, or external runtime dependencies. Static HTML must remain readable without JavaScript. Native controls, keyboard operation, accessible contrast and reduced-motion support are required. New entries must fit the existing data model without bespoke page layout.

The user approved a two-stage plan and authorized autonomous execution of the webpage plan after research, including publication. No further concept-selection gate is needed. The new project is independent of the FedRAMP prototype lab.
