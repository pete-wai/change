# Independent factual review

Reviewed the early, lead, middle, and recent candidate manifests against the underlying records. A release, scope of enforcement, or documented deployment is more useful than an announcement alone. Findings below distinguish release evidence, ongoing transitions, and claims still to remove or verify.

## Material corrections before publication

- **des-aes-transition:** The EFF archive records two different challenges. Deep Crack alone took 56 hours in July **1998**; Deep Crack plus distributed.net took **22 hours 15 minutes** in January 1999. Correct both summary and structured milestones. FIPS 197 was published in November 2001, effective May 26, 2002, and DES-related standards were withdrawn in May 2005. Withdrawal still allowed Triple DES for some applications; do not describe a single federal software migration date. [EFF records](https://w2.eff.org/Privacy/Crypto/Crypto_misc/DESCracker/), [NIST withdrawal](https://csrc.nist.gov/News/2005/Withdrawal-of-FIPS-46-3-FIPS-74-and-FIPS-81).
- **outlook-attachment-security:** Microsoft's May 15 release describes the new update as **blocking access** to several executable attachment types and adding prompts when programs send mail or access the address book. Save-before-opening describes an **earlier** update in the same release. Microsoft's then Office VP dates the new update's download to **June 8, 2000**; the earlier release only forecast the week of May 22. State the actual shipped behavior and date. Outlook 2002 integrated it, corroborating changed practice beyond the standalone announcement. [Announcement](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2000/05/15/microsoft-to-deliver-major-outlook-security-solution-to-help-protect-customers-against-computer-viruses/?ver=3.7.1), [Sinofsky account](https://hardcoresoftware.learningbyshipping.com/p/060-iloveyou), [Microsoft security bulletin](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security-updates/securitybulletins/2001/ms01-038).
- **exchange-online-basic-auth-retirement:** “All tenants” describes **affected protocols** only; explicitly exclude SMTP AUTH from this retirement. Microsoft still documents separately scheduled Basic Auth retirement for SMTP AUTH. Extension tenants were disabled starting in early 2023; December 31 was the self-service re-enable deadline, not a clean final enforcement date. Basic Auth sends a reusable username and password to the service; avoid an implication that those credentials necessarily went over unencrypted transport. [Service documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-nz/exchange/clients-and-mobile-in-exchange-online/deprecation-of-basic-authentication-exchange-online).
- **chrome-site-isolation / chrome-site-isolation-spectre:** May 29 is Chrome 67 stable release, not verified simultaneous activation for all users. July 11 engineering report says the feature had been enabled for **99%** of supported desktop users. Site Isolation was a preexisting project; Spectre raised its urgency. Chrome 67 limited sites sharing renderer memory; protection against a **fully compromised** renderer was additional Chrome 77 desktop work. Keep July evidence and one entry only. [July engineering rollout](https://security.googleblog.com/2018/07/mitigating-spectre-with-site-isolation.html?hl=el), [design history](https://www.chromium.org/developers/design-documents/site-isolation/).
- **office-activex-default-disabled:** Do not date both Office 2024 and Microsoft 365 to 2024. Microsoft directly confirms the 2024 LTSC default and separately confirms present default in both product lines. Use 2024 for Office LTSC 2024; omit the exact Microsoft 365 rollout date unless supported by a dated Microsoft release record. [LTSC documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/office/ltsc/2024/overview), [current support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/vba/activex-controls-are-disabled-by-default-in-microsoft-365-and-office-2024).
- **npm-trusted-publishing / pypi-oidc-provenance-transition:** npm's July 2025 release explicitly excludes provenance for private source repos and supports opting out; avoid “automatically attaches” without scope. Prefer retaining the distinct npm classic-token enforcement and one optional OIDC transition, not two overlapping optional entries. A registry's release proves availability, not material uptake. If retaining the cross-registry record, anchor it in **PyPI's 2023 launch** and distinguish PyPI attestations (default in the official publishing action unless disabled) from PyPI 2FA. [npm GA](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-07-31-npm-trusted-publishing-with-oidc-is-generally-available/), [registry comparison](https://github.blog/security/supply-chain-security/our-plan-for-a-more-secure-npm-supply-chain/), [PyPI security internals](https://docs.pypi.org/about/security-and-internals/).
- **memory-safe-language-transition:** Title generalizes beyond the evidence, all of which is Google. The firmware post is primarily a technical demonstration and should not establish a firmware-wide migration. Make the subject Android/Google and anchor changed practice in shipped Android code; the [October engineering report](https://security.googleblog.com/2024/10/safer-with-google-advancing-memory.html) documents adoption in sections of network, firmware and graphics stacks. Separate these shipped changes from longer-term strategy.
- **tls-10-11-browser-retirement:** Firefox 78 release notes confirm actual disablement. The cited February Chromium post says Chrome 83 was the *expected* milestone, and the other Chrome post later says *at least* Chrome 84; neither establishes the actual July/August rollout. Drop the Chrome 84/date claim unless adding a completion source. Firefox's confirmed June 30 release is sufficient to retain the case with narrower copy. [Firefox 78](https://www.firefox.com/en-US/firefox/78.0/releasenotes/), [Chrome postponed schedule](https://security.googleblog.com/2019/10/chrome-ui-for-deprecating-legacy-tls.html).
- **intrusion-detection-systems:** The listed 1999 bibliography link did not load, and the 2001 NIST primer's recommendation does not show broad deployment. Keep only with a documented deployment, or frame as “By 2001, NIST was explaining how organizations could add host and network monitoring” and defer if the project requires actual practice evidence. [NIST primer](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/31/final).
- **perimeter-firewalls:** The original sources are 2000/2002/2009 and don't substantiate a 1990 milestone on their own. A contemporary USENIX paper documents actual use of application gateways at AT&T, DEC and other organizations by 1992, citing AT&T's 1990 work; Cheswick and Bellovin's 1996 paper describes long-running deployment. Add those and title the date range as early 1990s rather than a universal 1990 adoption. [Chapman 1992](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/publications/library/proceedings/sec92/full_papers/chapman.pdf), [Cheswick and Bellovin 1996](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/publications/library/proceedings/sec96/full_papers/cheswick/index.html).
- **wep-wpa-transition:** The NIST technical guide confirms October 2002 publication of the interim WPA design and the WPA2 certification name. Neither that date nor IEEE publication proves customer adoption. Explain what shipped or was certified, or call it an offered replacement rather than an industry switch on a known date. Original Cornell link returned 404; replace with a readable authors' or conference copy. [NIST technical chronology, §5.1.1](https://csrc.nist.gov/files/pubs/sp/800/94/r1/ipd/docs/draft_sp800-94-rev1.pdf).
- **hsts:** RFC 6797 and Chromium's current documentation support the mechanism and first-visit limit, but not the candidate's 2009 major-site deployment. Drop that milestone or replace it with Chromium 12's documented 2011 preload support, which precedes the 2012 RFC. [Chromium 2011](https://blog.chromium.org/2011/06/new-chromium-security-features-june.html?hl=hu), [RFC 6797](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6797), [Chromium HSTS documentation](https://www.chromium.org/hsts/).

## Corrections already identified by the lead

- **certificate-transparency:** Remove the unsupported 2008 origin. RFC 6962 specifies the logs in 2013; anchor the practice in Chrome enforcement for publicly trusted certificates issued after April 30, 2018. Certificate Transparency detects misissuance; it does not prevent a CA from issuing. [RFC](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6962), [Chrome project](https://googlechrome.github.io/CertificateTransparency/).
- **ssl3-retirement:** POODLE disclosure in October 2014 preceded Firefox 34 disabling SSL 3.0 in December and the June 2015 RFC prohibition. “Browser changes followed” reverses part of that order. [Firefox 34](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/34.0/releasenotes/), [RFC 7568](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7568).
- **rc4-tls-retirement:** The February 2015 RFC is a client/server prohibition; Firefox 44's January 2016 removal grounds it in a shipped change. Do not imply all servers stopped at once. [RFC 7465](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7465), [Firefox 44 developer release record](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Mozilla/Firefox/Releases/44).
- **dns-forgery-resistance:** Replace dead disclosure slides with the July 8, 2008 coordinated disclosure and vendors' mitigations. Make the shipped resolver updates the 2008 response and RFC 5452 the 2009 formalization. The disclosure date is not necessarily the date of Kaminsky's public presentation. [CERT VU#800113](https://kb.cert.org/vuls/id/800113), [RFC 5452](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5452).
- **android-sandbox:** Current Android documentation establishes unique UID and kernel enforcement, not the asserted September 2008 release milestone or a displaced practice. Exclude as planned. **windows-dep / dep-nx-windows:** merge the duplicate; preserve the XP SP2 default OptIn coverage for essential Windows programs and services and the compatible-CPU limitation. Drop the dead AMD link or source the processor milestone separately. [Microsoft DEP policy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/troubleshoot/windows-client/performance/determine-hardware-dep-available).

## Remaining entries: retain with these qualifications

- **morris-worm-cert:** SEI's history and its CERT report independently of the candidate text establish the November worm, DARPA request and December formation. Avoid “first Internet incident center” without defining the scope; “The Morris worm leads to CERT/CC” is sufficient. The government report is a later estimate, not contemporaneous with 1988. [SEI history](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/history-of-innovation/fostering-growth-in-professional-cyber-incident-management/).
- **ssh-remote-login:** The creator's institutional history supports the sniffer incident and July 1995 SSH release; OpenSSH project history is the better authority for its fork history and OpenBSD integration. The RFC is published in 2006, and server authentication requires verifying the host key. “SSH gives administrators encrypted remote logins” avoids suggesting Telnet or rlogin disappeared. [SSH history](https://www.ssh.com/academy/ssh), [OpenSSH history](https://www.openssh.org/history.html), [RFC 4251](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4251.html).
- **microsoft-patch-cadence:** NIST's patch guide supports the previously available patches, not that these two worms directly caused Microsoft's monthly schedule. Keep customer predictability as the vendor's stated reason and the worms as context. [NIST SP 800-40](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication800-40.pdf), [Microsoft retrospective](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/msrc/blog/2013/10/10-years-of-update-tuesdays/).
- **windows-xp-sp2-firewall:** NIST §4.6 distinguishes the old default from SP2 and explicitly notes the third-party exception. August 6 describes release to manufacturers, not every consumer's installation. [NIST guide](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication800-69.pdf).
- **open-relays:** Sendmail 8.9 release note dates the changed configuration to May 19, 1998 and describes opting back into relay behavior. RFC 2505 documents relay abuse and warns that anti-relay rules alone do not prevent spam. Safe if title/body do not imply all servers switched that year. [Release notes](https://www.sendmail.org/~ca/email/releases/8.9.0.release.html), [RFC 2505](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2505.html).
- **lets-encrypt:** Public beta removed invitations; nearly 192 million is the operator's February 2020 website count, not customers, installations or its cumulative billion certificates. [Beta](https://letsencrypt.org/2015/12/03/entering-public-beta.html), [reported use](https://letsencrypt.org/2020/02/27/one-billion-certs.html).
- **sha1-certificates:** Firefox 51 ramp was underway before the public collision; Mozilla said the phase-out would reach all Firefox users February 24. Specify **publicly trusted certificate chains**; manually imported roots were explicitly exempted. [Enforcement](https://blog.mozilla.org/security/2017/02/23/the-end-of-sha-1-on-the-public-web/), [private-root exception](https://blog.mozilla.org/security/2016/10/18/phasing-out-sha-1-on-the-public-web/).
- **http-warning:** Google said 81 of the top 100 sites used HTTPS *by default* before Chrome 68; July 24 is stable release and the start of desktop rollout, not completion on every platform. [Security notice](https://security.googleblog.com/2018/02/a-secure-web-is-here-to-stay.html), [release record](https://chromereleases.googleblog.com/2018/07/).
- **breached-passwords:** Research reports 26% of warnings resulted in a new password among study extension users, not 26% of all users. The mechanism compares compromised username/password pairs, not a password in isolation. [Research report](https://research.google/pubs/protecting-accounts-from-credential-stuffing-with-password-breach-alerting/).
- **flash:** Adobe's working EOL page confirms Dec. 31 support end and Jan. 12 blocking in Flash Player. Google's 80% and 17% refer to desktop Chrome users who visited a site containing Flash each day, not direct evidence they played Flash content. [Adobe EOL](https://www.adobe.com/products/flashplayer/end-of-life.html), [Chrome telemetry](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/chrome/saying-goodbye-flash-chrome/).
- **mass-mfa:** Google reported auto-enrolling over 150 million *people* and a 50% decrease in compromised accounts; the post does not specify the detailed measurement design or establish the same effect for any other provider. Keep attribution. [Google rollout report](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/safety-security/safer-internet-day-2022/).
- **internet-macros:** Rollout dates agree with Microsoft; qualify “emailed document” to affected Windows Office apps and files carrying Mark of the Web. Trusted locations and trusted documents can differ. [Microsoft rollout and decision flow](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-apps/security/internet-macros-blocked).
- **s3-public-access:** Both 2023 changes applied to new buckets only. The 2018 AWS record distinguishes privacy by default from new guardrails; April 5 was the beginning, April 27 completion. [AWS guardrail announcement and update](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/amazon-s3-block-public-access-another-layer-of-protection-for-your-accounts-and-buckets/).
- **http-permission:** October 21 report confirms default extension beyond Enhanced Protection. It concerns public-site navigations where HTTPS fails, retains approval until a site goes unvisited for two weeks, and excludes sites that cannot obtain a publicly trusted certificate by default. Do not claim permission for all HTTP traffic or for every visit. [Google rollout report](https://blog.google/security/chrome-now-warns-before-insecure-http-connections/).
- **windows-uac:** Vista split-token/elevation behavior and compatibility virtualization are distinct. Limit virtualization to eligible legacy applications and note credentials for standard users vs. consent for admins. Use 2006–07 if both business and retail release are presented. [Microsoft UAC history and behavior](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/secauthz/user-account-control).
- **usb-autorun:** Specify the AutoRun-supplied task disappearing from AutoPlay for non-optical removable media, not all AutoPlay or all USB execution. Microsoft's advisory dates KB971029 availability August 2009 and automatic-update offering February 2011 for older systems. Optical emulation is an exception. [Microsoft advisory 967940](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security-updates/securityadvisories/2009/967940).
- **chromium-sandbox:** Launch-era source supports rendering process mediation for file/network access and intent to contain compromise. Scope to Chrome/Chromium; do not conflate this with later per-site process isolation or claim the sandbox blocks web phishing. [Chromium design explanation](https://blog.chromium.org/2008/09/security-architecture.html).
- **android-runtime-permissions:** Applies to dangerous permissions and runtime requests for apps targeting API 23+, not all app permissions. “When the app asks for it” is more accurate than implying the platform infers when the app genuinely needs it. [Android 6.0 changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/marshmallow/android-6.0-changes).
- **gatekeeper:** Apple confirms Developer ID, signature/integrity check, and identified developer default. Describe downloaded software, allow user overrides, and distinguish Apple identity from safety review. June release forecast does not prove exact July 25 delivery; cite actual July availability release if preserving the day. [Apple technical overview](https://www.apple.com/media/us/osx/2012/docs/OSX_MountainLion_Core_Technologies_Overview.pdf).
- **npapi-retirement:** Firefox 52 actually removed non-Flash NPAPI support, including Java and Silverlight; scope to Firefox in the title if other browser launches remain unsupported. A separately documented Chrome deprecation could extend the story. [Firefox 52](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/52.0/releasenotes/).
- **smbv1-clean-install-removal:** Cite server/client distinctions: Windows 10 1709 Home and Pro omitted server but retained client on clean installs, subject to disuse removal; Windows 10 Pro 1809 omitted client; Windows 11 clean installs omit both. Do not claim WannaCry originated the SMBv1 removal plan. [Microsoft edition matrix](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-server/storage/file-server/Troubleshoot/smbv1-not-installed-by-default-in-windows).
- **passkeys-default-signin:** Google made it the default **option/prompt** for supported personal Google Accounts, retaining passwords. Google reported over one billion uses across **over** 400 million Google Accounts. “Device-held” should allow credentials synced through a passkey provider, not imply one device only. [Google release](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/passkeys-week-is-here/), [usage report](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/safety-security/google-passkeys-update-april-2024/).
- **github-contributor-mandatory-2fa:** Nearly 95% is the notified 2023 cohort; 54% is a *relative increase* in adoption across active contributors, not percentage points or total enrollment. [GitHub report](https://github.blog/security/supply-chain-security/securing-millions-of-developers-through-2fa/).
- **pypi-mandatory-2fa:** January 1 policy date and current mandatory status are supported. In 2019 PyPI began the path by adding 2FA; don't say broad enforcement already began then. Authenticated uploads use API tokens or trusted publishing; downloading does not require an account. [PyPI notice](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2023-12-13-2fa-enforcement/), [current internals](https://docs.pypi.org/about/security-and-internals/).
- **aws-imdsv2-transition:** November 2023 Quick Starts is confirmed (“starting today”); mid-2024 newly released types was still prospective in the cited post. Drop the precise June claim unless verifying a dated launch or general documentation of actual application. “New console Quick Start launches required IMDSv2 by default” is sufficient evidence of practice. [AWS rollout](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/amazon-ec2-instance-metadata-service-imdsv2-by-default/).
- **gmail-sender-requirements:** One-click unsubscribe applies to marketing/promotional or subscribed mail, not transactional mail. The detailed FAQ calls the bulk threshold close to 5,000/day per primary domain, persistent once reached. November 2025 is a ramp, not universal instant rejection; personal Gmail scope is correct. [FAQ](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14229414).
- **android-unused-permission-reset:** Sort under 2020 if the entry leads with Android 11 introduction; in the original implementation auto-reset applied after months of disuse for apps targeting Android 11 or later. The later expansion requires separate dated evidence. [Android 11 documentation](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/11/privacy/permissions).
- **npm-classic-token-revocation:** Dec. 9 completion confirms revocation and new npm login session behavior. Keep exact scope for granular write tokens and note opt-in 2FA bypass for noninteractive CI. July OIDC release was separate. [Completion notice](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-12-09-npm-classic-tokens-revoked-session-based-auth-and-cli-token-management-now-available/).

## Additions from the canonical selection

- **early-pc-antivirus-scanning:** Keep the bounded market emergence claim. The [1989 NIST guide, pp. 3-2 and 5-5](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication500-166.pdf) recommends testing new software and using antivirus before releasing it to others; this is guidance. The [1992 NIST guide, §§1–1.2](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication800-5.pdf) specifically discusses widely available tools and personal computer products. [ESET's working NZ retrospective](https://www.eset.com/nz/about/30-years-it-security-anniversary/) corroborates its 1987 NOD32 predecessor and 1990 first Austrian STOPVIR retail sales; use it instead of the Singapore path. The [NIST archival mirror](https://csrc.nist.rip/publications/nistir/threats/subsubsection3_3_1_1.html) dates Brain to 1986 and calls it a resident boot-sector virus. These are evidence of a new tool category and real retail activity; they do not connect Brain specifically to the NOD precursor or establish industry penetration.
- **internet-wide-scanning:** Keep. [Shodan’s founder retrospective](https://blog.shodan.io/happy-10-year-anniversary/) supports a 2009 launch (it contains an obvious typo on the exact day’s year, so use just 2009), searchable metadata, and subsequent network-monitoring use. The [2013 USENIX study](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity13/technical-sessions/paper/durumeric) bounds the demonstrated speed to a single machine near gigabit Ethernet limits. Use “scan for a single service across public IPv4 addresses in under 45 minutes,” not all services/addresses or every network. The [authors’ 2024 follow-up](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15585v1) documents over 300 research papers, products from more than a dozen security companies, and attackers’ use. Name dual use in public copy and consider foregrounding exposure monitoring as the changed practice.
- **intrusion-detection-systems (resolution evidence):** Zeek’s project history records **operational** Bro deployment at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory in 1996 and the [1998 original publication](https://static.usenix.org/publications/library/proceedings/sec98/paxson.html). This is sufficient for a bounded real-use claim. Suggested anchor: “In 1996, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory deployed Bro to watch network traffic for signs of intrusion. By 2001, NIST was helping organizations choose host and network intrusion detection tools.” Avoid prevalence claims without measurements. [Zeek project history](https://old.zeek.org/manual/2.5.5/intro/index.html).
- **Additional primary evidence:** [PCI DSS v1.2.1 §4.1.1](https://listings.pcisecuritystandards.org/minisite/en/docs/pci_dss_v1-2.pdf) provides a concrete WEP retirement deadline for wireless networks transmitting cardholder data or connected to that environment: new deployments after March 31, 2009, and existing deployments after June 30, 2010. It is a scoped compliance requirement, not an observed count of compliant sites. The [Stubblefield, Ioannidis, and Rubin paper](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Using-the-Fluhrer-Mantin-and-Shamir-Attack-to-Break-WEP-Adam-Stubbefild.pdf) describes implementing the Fluhrer–Mantin–Shamir attack and recovering a key on a real network with permission. The [Apple July 25 announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2012/07/25Mountain-Lion-Available-Today-From-the-Mac-App-Store/) confirms actual Mountain Lion availability with Gatekeeper. The [Android 13 engineering report](https://security.googleblog.com/2022/12/memory-safe-languages-in-android-13.html?hl=cs) documents shipped memory-safe code in 2022, including Keystore2 and the Ultra-wideband stack; about 21% of newly added native code was Rust. Keep the denominator and attribute the figure to Google.
